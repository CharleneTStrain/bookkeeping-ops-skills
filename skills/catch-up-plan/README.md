# Catch-up plan

**The problem:** You're months behind on your books and someone else just set the deadline — a tax date, an IRS letter, a lender, your accountant. The pile is too big to start, and a bookkeeper's quote feels like a guess.

**What it does:** Starts with two questions and one 10-minute task, then works backwards from your deadline. Checks that your last finished month actually matches your last tax return, sets up categories before sorting, sizes the backlog in hours, orders the months by what the deadline needs, and checks revenue from Stripe, Square or PayPal the way that trips most catch-ups up. Shows doing it yourself vs. hiring help side by side, with the questions to ask any bookkeeper. For S-corps, checks for owner payroll first (December 31 is the last day to pay this year's owner wages), sets up distributions in place of owner draws, and sorts every owner payment into wages, distributions, reimbursements, loans or contributions. Sends anything about what you'll owe to your CPA instead of guessing.

**Use it with:** your deadline, the last month you're confident is finished, and a list of the accounts money moves through. Rough transaction counts are fine.

**You get:** one next step for today, plus an Excel workbook with a one-page summary, your accounts, a month-by-month backlog, a week-by-week plan that finishes before your deadline, a CPA questions list, and a routine to stay caught up. It doubles as an intake packet if you hire help. A planning tool, not tax advice.
